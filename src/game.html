Sectional Run

Frederick (FDK) → Bay Bridge (W29) · real airspace

You've just departed Frederick Muni in N421AP, headed for Bay Bridge on Kent Island. The plane holds 110 knots on its own, so you only steer and pick altitudes. Nothing stalls and nothing crashes. The challenge is reading the chart. The direct line runs straight through the Washington DC SFRA, so you'll need to plan around it, slip under the Baltimore Bravo shelves, and answer checkride questions along the way.

  • ◀ ▶ (hold) set the heading bug, and the plane turns to it at standard rate · ▲ ▼ change target altitude by 500 ft
  • INST shows draggable flight instruments, as a six-pack or a GLASS EADI and EHSI (switch in the panel's top bar). Turn its HDG knob to set the bug, or tap the knob to sync it to your heading
  • RADIO call Potomac Approach, a tower, or self-announce on the CTAF
  • 3D shows the airspace as stacked slabs · TAC swaps in the real FAA chart
  • Tap anything on the map to find out what it is · pinch or − + to zoom · drag to look around
  • NAV opens the touchscreen navigator: GS, TRK, DIS and DTK, a moving map, the flight plan, nearest airports and Direct-To
  • Keyboard: ← → heading bug, ↑ ↓ altitude, R radio, V 3D, C chart, X speed, N navigator, Q questions on/off, P pause, L land

Terrain and MEFs come from AWS Terrain Tiles (USGS 3DEP, SRTM and others, via Mapzen). Airspace, airports, obstacles and VORs come from FAA ADDS data. Water and towns are traced from the FAA Baltimore–Washington TAC. The SFRA and FRZ are approximate rings. This is a practice game: not for navigation, and no substitute for current charts, NOTAMs and SFRA training.
